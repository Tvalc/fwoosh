## 1. LOCKED DECISIONS

1. Diep Loc is the Earth lead and Vietnam's Champion. POV home base.
2. Cuong lives. He is Diep's Companion, mechanically subordinate to his younger sister. The sibling duo is a core engine of the book.
3. Duy Duong dies permanently in the first Atlantis encounter (Chapter 7). His death establishes permadeath with blood.

6. Chit-tat-to is the NPC co-lead with his own POV chapters, party, and arc.
7. The Tower is CUT, replaced by the Partition (Section 4.5): Earth's ~195 national claims plus Atlantis's 7 regions, contested in simulacra at the Adjudication Grounds before integration day. The world map is the scoreboard and the season engine.
8. The Anacortes/JWST material is shelved. The le'Greuse prologue is cut. The Atlantis-surfacing concept survives as a one-page cold-open flash-forward.

## 3. LOGLINE AND PITCH

**Logline:** When a Ho Chi Minh City market massacre kills her whole world, a 19-year-old martial artist is recruited by desperate gods to fight for humanity's survival on a refugee supercontinent about to crash into the Pacific. Her first act as Earth's champion is an atrocity. Her only way forward is to repay it, alongside the last survivor of the people she helped kill.

**The two-protagonist pitch:** Diep climbs the ladder every LitRPG reader knows: levels, classes, a tower, a countdown. Chit-tat-to climbs a ladder no one has written: his species is rated "barely sentient" by the System itself, his people read their own status screens as crude pictures, and he is the first Ratkin whose screen begins rendering in words. Diep is fighting to save a world. Chit-tat-to is fighting to be counted as a person. The stories braid.

**The DCC-lesson checklist this design satisfies:**
- Co-lead as strong as the protagonist (Chit-tat-to = the Donut slot, played for pathos-first instead of vanity-first)
- System with personality (see Section 6)
- Dark comedy amid real violence, stakes that never reset
- Found family: a party held together by guilt, debt, and stubborn love
- Audio-first design: every System message written to be performed
- Escalating structure with clean season breaks (the Tower)

## 4. WORLD RULES

### 4.1 The Situation
Two thousand years ago, the being Earth knows as Adonai sealed Earth's universe into a pocket dimension to protect humanity from the multiverse. Pocket universes decay. Earth's has roughly one year left. When it fails, Earth reintegrates: the Sakti (ambient life-force that powers magic and the System) floods the planet, animals ascend toward sentience, and every sentient being gets a status screen. Most of humanity is projected to die in the transition. This projection is delivered by the System in the tone of an actuary.

Atlantis is the other pocket-dimension experiment: a 136-million-square-kilometer circular supercontinent built a millennium ago by Elven and Dwarven gods as a refugee camp for dying worlds. Seven wedge-shaped regions, one per species-group: Humans (kingdom of Leath, arrived 100 years ago), Elves, Dwarves, Goblinkind, Nijiit (feline), Gnomes, and the shared Wylac/Kilar ocean region. Five hundred years of near-constant border war. At the center: an 8-million-square-kilometer no-man's land of the worst monsters of every world, and at its heart, the Tower.

When Earth's pocket fails, Atlantis reintegrates too, surfacing in the middle of the Pacific. Earth gets magic, monsters, AND five billion armed refugees with a millennium of grudges, on the same day.

**Lore delivery rule: nothing above is ever delivered as a document.** It is discovered: a border skirmish witnessed, a Leath politician's speech, a Ratkin pictogram history-wall, a drunk dwarf's complaint. The draft's read-along infodump is banned.

### 4.2 The Champion Program
Adonai and Odin (and analogous patron gods worldwide; Vietnam's are the Four Immortals) recruit the newly and violently dead. The offer: a second life as a Champion or Companion on Atlantis. Mission: establish a human foothold, ally with at least one major faction, and climb the Tower to win resources, legitimacy, and power before integration day.

- **Champions** hold the quest, make binding decisions, gain faster progression, and bear the penalties. One primary Champion per national slot.
- **Companions** are bound to a Champion. Slower base progression, but they share party experience and can hold roles Champions cannot (a Companion can betray no one; the System enforces it, which is its own horror).
- Prior champion cohorts have gone missing, gone native, or gone dark. The Italians (special forces, perfect scores) vanished two years ago. The Burundi/Malawi group went into Leath politics. This is a mystery thread, not backstory.

### 4.3 Death and the Favor Economy
- Death is permanent for champions, companions, and natives alike.
- Gods can resurrect, at catastrophic cost to the power reserves keeping Earth's pocket stable. Every resurrection granted shortens Earth's countdown. The gods will almost never do it, and when one finally does (late series), it should bankrupt something.
- **Divine Favor** is the currency between mortals and patrons: earned by completing quests, honoring a patron's values, and impressive deeds; spent on blessings, information, emergency intervention, and appeals. Favor debts (like the party's -100 with Odin) function as moral liens. The Blood Debt questline (Section 8) is how the party works theirs off.

### 4.4 The System
- Universal interface: status, skills, quests, party mechanics, notifications. **Canonical framing: the System calls a status screen an "account," and defines it as a record of your body, your deeds, and your debts.** This framing debuts in the cold open and recurs; it ties the interface to the ledger motif (Blood Debt, favor liens, the attrition ledger) that runs through the whole series.
- Attributes: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Luck. HP = 10x CON. MP = 5x INT.
- Skills: Novice (1-20), Apprentice (20-40), Journeyman, Expert, Master. Tier-ups grant a modifier or linked skill.
- Classes are earned through Trials, ranked Common / Rare / Epic / Legendary.
- Earth skills translate (Vovinam, police training, lockpicking, cooking). This is Vietnam's edge and the book's flavor.
- Firearms are "Forbidden" grade: outside multiverse physics, devastating, finite. Ammunition does not respawn. The guns are a melting ice cube, and the book treats each magazine as a strategic resource until they're gone (target: dry by end of Act 2, forcing full conversion to System-native combat).
- **Sentience is a stat the System tracks.** Ratkin sit at the threshold. This is the co-lead's entire arc made mechanical.

### 4.5 The Partition (replaces the Tower)
When Earth integrates, every faction in the multiverse wants a piece of a defenseless Eden. The gods' leverage bought one concession: adjudication before invasion. Earth's territory is divided into **claims** (its ~195 nations plus the 7 regions of Atlantis itself), each of which can be formally contested before integration day.

- **Contests happen in simulacra.** The Adjudication Grounds at the center of the no-man's land instantiate each contested region as a rendered zone, populated with the exact forces slated to enter it on integration day. Earth's champions defend copies of their homelands.
- **Win:** the region's integration terms are written in humanity's favor (sanctuaries, protective Sakti infrastructure, gentler beast ascension, population blessings). **Lose:** the claim transfers to the winner, and the horde assigned to that region is reinforced and pre-positioned. The whole map cashes out at once on integration day.
- **The world map is the scoreboard.** Claims resolve green or red across the series, in scheduled adjudication waves (campaign seasons). Readers track the map; the map is the serial engine.
- **Compression:** most of Earth's champion teams are dead, missing, or gone native. Undefended claims forfeit unless another nation's team stands proxy. Whose homeland do you defend when you cannot defend them all: the series' central strategic question and the Blood Debt engine at planetary scale.
- **Contest format is MMO battleground design**, not dungeon-crawl floors: multi-phase objectives (capture and hold, escort, siege) with a **persistent attrition ledger**: damage, deaths, and resource spend carry across phases, no resets. Zone design adapts the Ring Space drifting-objective framework and Broken Clock boss timing where fitting.
- **The discarded races (Ratkin among them) have no claim standing anywhere, by law.** Chit-tat-to's series goal made concrete: win standing first, then win a region. The villain space: whoever profits from the discarded staying discarded.
- Rival claimants include other worlds' champion cohorts, Atlantis factions, and claims-broker consortia fielding hired or enslaved proxies (Mei's pit thread plugs in here).

Selection: each nation confirms one Champion of standing. The count of confirmed defenders at series start is 201, and it is shrinking.

### 4.6 The Wall's Ledger (Adonai's debts; a story-generation engine)
Canon principle: the seal Adonai built around Earth is the root system under the series' systemic problems. When a condition needs a cause, check the wall first.
1. **The lost century** (canon, cold open): worlds receive ~100 years of integration notice; Earth's was spent hidden. "He is sorry."
2. **The leak** (canon, Section 7.8): the decaying wall seeped Sakti for roughly two decades; sensitives worldwide were damaged and gifted by the same exposure.
3. **The starved pantheons:** the wall throttled divine connection to Earth for two millennia. Miracles stopped and gods went silent because they were cut off; Earth's pantheons nearly faded. Odin's bitterness and the gods' visible fear are wall-caused. The divine grudge engine: Adonai's brother gods paid for his choice.
4. **The queue at the Arch:** the wall throttled soul processing; Earth's dead wait in a line generations deep. Locked thread: Miss Hue's husband, dead ~30 years, may still be in that line. Her patron is the god whose wall built the queue. Planted in Ch 3.
5. **Level 1, almost everyone:** two millennia without Sakti left humanity mechanically stunted relative to every other integrating world. The wall made them weak the day it was built to keep them safe.
6. **The compressed ascension:** Earth's fauna ascends in a spike rather than a curve because ascension was dammed for two thousand years.
7. **The entangled pockets:** the Elven and Dwarven gods modeled Atlantis's pocket partly on Adonai's (draft canon, now locked as consequence). The pockets are entangled; his wall's decay destabilized theirs; Atlantis crashing into Earth's Pacific, specifically, flows downhill from his choice.
8. **The spent war chest:** maintaining the wall drained the gods' reserves. This is why resurrection is ruinous, intervention is rationed, and the champion program is five dead people from Phu Nhuan instead of an army.

**The limit (binding):** the wall explains systemic conditions, never personal evil. Villains choose: the claims-brokers, Nam Cam's network, and the orderly are chosen evils, not wall-caused. Monocausality flattens a series; Adonai's guilt is the theology, not the excuse.
**Endgame note (not locked):** the god who built the wall paying the series' one ruinous resurrection is a candidate for the final entry in his ledger. Decide much later.

## 5. STAT SCREEN DISCIPLINE (COMMERCIAL RULE SET)

1. Maximum one full status screen per chapter, and only when a number changes the scene.
2. Notifications are dialogue from a character (the System), never database output. If a notification isn't doing character work or comedy work, cut it.
3. Never repeat a notification format twice in one chapter.
4. Skill flavor text is a punchline budget: one per chapter.
5. All System text written to be performed aloud (audio-first). Read every message in a flat bureaucrat voice; if it isn't funny or chilling, rewrite.
6. Numbers matter narratively or not at all. "Diep's health dropped below a third" beats "Diep took 47 damage" unless 47 is the joke.
7. **Achievements pay on the page.** Every achievement grants visible, concrete rewards at the moment of issue: stat awards, and where earned, unique skills, powers, or items. The System never withholds reward specifications ("to be disclosed" is banned); the Auditor discloses with precision, and its menace lives in HOW it discloses, never in hidden mechanics. Mystery belongs to implications ("This is a gift, and is also not"), not to concealed numbers. Unique-grade achievements state their uniqueness explicitly ("This will never be awarded again"), because scarcity shown beats scarcity implied.

## 6. THE SYSTEM'S VOICES (TWO, BY DESIGN)

The comedy engine, split to avoid DCC's lane while delivering the genre's fanfare.

**Voice one: THE AUDITOR (records, notifications, screens).** A vast, ancient, rule-bound bureaucracy with a buried conscience. Dry, precise, procedurally cruel, occasionally and involuntarily humane. Never uses exclamation points. Never lies, but chooses disclosures, and the choices are characterization. Mercy must be deniable. Its menace lives in HOW it discloses, never in hidden mechanics.

**Voice two: THE MAGISTRATE (presiding voice of contested proceedings; working name).** Not a game-show host and not a crier: a TV-court judge at multiverse scale, Judge Judy and Judge Mathis energy, the judiciary as personality. Self-aggrandizing, interrupting, moralizing from a bench nine millennia deep, openly contemptuous of litigants, and, infuriatingly, usually technically correct. Presides over claim contests at the Adjudication Grounds (debuts Act 3); the broadcasts exist because rulings require witnesses, and the Magistrate performs justice the way TV judges do, as a mockery of what justice is supposed to look like that never once breaks procedure. Sample register: "Nine millennia on this bench and champions still believe grief is an argument." / "This court has outlived four pantheons who assumed its patience was negotiable." / "I know what you did. You know what you did. We will not waste the ledger's time pretending otherwise." **Hard limits (anti-DCC): no sponsors, no ratings, no viewer economy. Nobody profits from watching, and that is somehow worse.** Friction with the Auditor is court-flavored: the clerk resents the bench's editorializing ("The presiding remarks are not the System's. The record reflects that.").

**JUDGMENT AND PRECEDENT MECHANICS (binding; replaces fanfare):** notifications do not celebrate, they RULE. Every award is a finding; significant awards carry editorial weight and, above all, PRECEDENT. Notable achievements cite prior recipients and their subsequent fates ("Most recent prior recipient: Szel of Vekh-Amun, subsequently First Navigator of the Unbroken Fleet. Outcomes are not guaranteed."), which is the series' legend-building engine, the Anarchist's Cookbook slot: recurring legendary names accrete across citations, prior recipients become aspirational or cautionary lore, and readers learn the multiverse through boilerplate. "Outcomes are not guaranteed" is the canonical disclaimer. Failures, deaths, and debts still arrive in silence and fine print, no editorial, no precedent, and the reader learns to fear the quiet screens.

**OFFICES (generalization of the precedent engine):** Unique-grade titles carry duties as well as buffs; the award is also an appointment. Title-holders across worlds form living registries the court can call upon (the 1,406 First Witnesses are a witness registry; the vindicated-disbelieved staff the Unheard Docket, Section 7.8). Precedent citations are therefore not trivia but a NETWORK: prior recipients are colleagues, predecessors, and future allies or problems, and the legends the citations build can walk onstage.

**ORIGIN CANON (known premise; nature unanswered until series-late):** an ancient civilization, working name THE AUTHORS, set out to remove injustice from the galaxy and built the System as its enforcement entity. It applies justice through THEIR moral code, complete, consistent, and merciless, because the Authors held mercy to be favoritism, an inequality before the law. The Authors are gone; the code runs on. Everything hateable about the System is someone's sincere idea of fairness, which is the thesis with its origin attached.

**CULTURAL MISMATCH MECHANICS (the Carl's-boxers slot; Author values fossilized as rules):** absurd buffs and debuffs descend from the Authors' moral code, and nobody alive knows why. Canon starters: **Honest Feet** (the Authors held that the honest walk unshod; barefoot acts of testimony, oath, and combat gain a truth-and-honor bonus, which Vovinam-trained barefoot fighters trigger constantly by accident, and which the party discovers before understanding). **Clean Hands** (washing before a petition grants a small bonus to appeals; the doctrine is literal). **Contempt of Court** (dismissing or interrupting a System screen before it finishes rendering incurs a micro-fine: "Reading time is court time."). **Salt Oath** (contracts sworn over shared salt bind at a higher grade; Author hospitality law, never explained). Add sparingly, one per arc at most, always discovered in play, never info-dumped.

**THE ARGUMENT (why we hate our System; the book's thesis, distinct from DCC):** DCC's engine is suffering as content. Ours is suffering as PROCEDURE. The System is a court that is never wrong and never just: perfect records, zero mercy. Grief is filed under aggravating factors, a massacre becomes a title, a homeland becomes a claim number, and the debts you owe are calculated to the soul while the debts owed to YOU are unenforceable, because standing is granted, not possessed, and the discarded have none. Vietnam recognizes this machine, because colonization arrived as surveyors and land courts before it arrived as soldiers, and the System is the surveyor of the multiverse. Every System beat in the book serves this argument: the Auditor is the honest clerk of an obscene jurisdiction, the Herald is its ceremony, the gods are its landlord-litigants, and Chit-tat-to's arc is the discarded suing existence for personhood. We never hate the System for enjoying our pain. We hate it because it processes our pain correctly, and correctness is not justice.

**The relationship (the engine):** one System, two voices that visibly endure each other: the clerk and the bench. The Auditor's records are exact and unadorned; the Magistrate's rulings are performances the Auditor is required to transcribe and plainly resents. Their friction is renewable comedy that no single-voiced System runs.

**Sample messages (Auditor register, canonical):**

> **Quest Failed: Save the Ratkin.**
> Cause of failure: you shot them.
> Favor with Odin: -100.
> This entry is permanent. So are they.

> Your party has been awarded 700 experience.
> Experience is awarded without regard to circumstance.
> This is not the System's favorite rule either.

> **Title Earned: Caedite Eos.**
> Awarded for the elimination of 24 hostiles.
> 19 of the 24 have been reclassified: non-combatant.
> Reclassification does not affect the award. Titles, like history, are written by the survivors.

> Skill increased: Short Blades, Level 21.
> You are now measurably better at this than your brother.
> He has been notified.

> Debuff acquired: **Blood Debt.**
> This debuff cannot be dispelled, resisted, prayed away, or outrun.
> It can only be repaid.
> Current balance: 19.

**Sample lines (Herald register, for the Grounds):** "SEVEN REGIONS AND HONORED GUESTS OF ELEVEN WORLDS, tell them what they're fighting for!" / "A brand new integration clause, sanctuaries INCLUDED, gods and monsters, LOOK at those terms!" / "Contestants, remember: the ledger carries, the crowd remembers, and somebody is going home green."

**Rules for both:** the Auditor never exclaims; the Herald never stops. Long game: who wrote the System, why the Auditor keeps a rule it hates, and what the Herald is under the glitter are series-scale mysteries. Do not answer in Book 1.

### 7.1 Diep Loc, 19 (aged up from 17) — Earth Lead, Champion of Vietnam
Second-year university student. Vovinam weapons champion who beat her cop brother in open competition (canon, and a permanent running joke the System participates in). Wound: her best friend put a knife in her stomach at a market stall; her city, her parents' world, and her partner-in-crime brother's partner are gone; and the first thing she did with her second life was participate in a massacre. Arc, Book 1: from little sister to commander. She did not ask to outrank Cuong; the System made her Champion and the book makes her earn it. Her weapon skills translate spectacularly; her judgment has to catch up. Voice: dry, watchful, faster to act than to speak, sharper humor than anyone expects.

**Competence rule (binding, from Tony's note):** Pre-Atlantis Diep is a sport athlete, never an operator. Her reflexes read ring-trained (fast, technical, guard up, and slightly absurd outside a ring), never tactical (no weapon staging, no threat tracking, no clearing rooms, no knife on the nightstand). She is also a 19-year-old with ordinary insecurities: new friendships matter too much, her mother's opinions land, she rehearses conversations. The hardening happens ON the page, earned through Atlantis chapter by chapter; if she reads hardened before the hardening, the arc is broken. The same rule applies in reverse to Cuong: he has real tactical training, and the contrast between her ring instincts and his procedure is a renewable source of both comedy and grief.

**The step-up arc (locked, from Tony's design):** she grew up in her brother's shadow and was catching up before the massacre, but not confidently; her anxiety dreams rewrite wins as losses. On Atlantis the court inverts everything: she holds Champion rank, he carries crippling Judgments, and if she does not step up and lead, he dies. Her leadership is born as a legal act (she signs Standing Surety for him first, before she believes in her own signature) and then must become real, decision by decision, while the whole party grows by carrying a man they love. His parallel arc: the protector learning to be protected. Her scoreboard psyche transforms across the book from fighting for points nobody keeps into keeping the only score that matters, his balance falling.

**Class: THE FOUNDER (Rare at acquisition, Legendary growth path).** Diep fights as a Vovinam weapons master; the class's unique mechanic is **Transmission**: she can formalize her art as a System-recognized School. Anyone who takes the **Disciple's Oath** enters it, and the trade runs both ways:
- At a disciple's class selection, Diep **co-authors** the outcome. She does not pick their class (agency theft flattens the cast and reviewers punish it); she unlocks options one full rank above what the disciple could reach alone, drawn from the School's technique pool plus the disciple's own history. The disciple chooses. Sworn followers get strictly better classes AND stay people.
- **Costs:** a disciple's class is School-bound; leaving the School weakens it. Every oath is a genuine decision, every departure is a story. And Diep carries command liability: her disciples' deeds and deaths land on her favor ledger. Command responsibility, mechanized (Griffin register in system form).
- **Cast consequences:** Cuong swears first; the System enjoys the rank inversion. Mei begs to swear as penance; Diep refuses to accept the oath for most of Book 1 (trust, mechanized). Chit-tat-to refuses fealty outright, because the fastest road to power runs through an oath to the people who killed his mother, and he will not kneel for it. What he negotiates instead, late in Book 1, is a **Compact between equals**, which becomes the legal instrument of Ratkin standing under the Partition.
- **Strategic layer:** under the Partition, a School can train claim defenders at scale. The Founder class is humanity's export technology. **Lineage (locked):** the class is the direct descendant of Nguyễn Lộc's founding of Vovinam under occupation, a system built so a threatened people need not borrow their defense; Miss Hue, trained in his earliest classes, is the living link, and she names the lineage when the School is founded.

### 7.2 Chit-tat-to, 15 — NPC Co-Lead
Ratkin adolescent, sole survivor of the cell-block community. CONFIRMED: his mother, one of the four Elders killed and keeper of the village history wall, is among the dead. **Anti-shallow rules:** no flashback grief montages, ever. She appears through inherited obligations, not memories: he inherits the history wall and must record the massacre himself, in pictograms, including the humans he now travels with. The Blood Debt ledger lists all 19 dead individually by name and pictogram; every repayment the System credits lands against a specific person, never a number. Grief stays procedural, specific, and current. The System rates Ratkin "barely sentient"; they read status screens as pictograms. When the humans' party bond touches him, his screen begins, occasionally, rendering words. He is the first of his kind to read the multiverse's opinion of his kind. Arc, Book 1: from survivor to founder. He does not forgive the humans. He does something harder: he invoices them. The Blood Debt is his to collect, and collecting it means making them help him build what his people were never allowed: a settlement, a name, a seat at a table. He recruits his own party from Atlantis's discarded (a gremlin exile, an oath-broken hobgoblin, a Kilar stranded a thousand miles from water; finalize roster in character pass). His chapters carry the Brooks register: warmth, wonder, fable-logic, and a knife of grief under all of it. Voice on page: initially pictogram-thought rendered as simple, vivid concrete images; grows in complexity as his sentience stat climbs. His language acquisition IS his leveling. By the time he speaks fluent Vietnamese-inflected Common in late Book 1, the reader has watched a person assemble himself.

### 7.3 Cuong Loc, 25 — Companion, the Shield
HCMC cop. His SOU training killed 19 noncombatants in the dark; his partner died in front of him a chapter later. Arc: unlearning Earth. The instincts that made him good at his job are exactly wrong here, and his sister outranks him by divine decree. He becomes the party's shield, in both class and function: the man who puts himself between. His relationship with Chit-tat-to is the slowest thaw in the book and the most important one; he is the face of what was done. Voice: understated, procedural, the Griffin register in human form. His jokes are so dry other characters miss them.

### 7.4 Mei Nhuan, 18 — the Blade with a Debt
Thief from a family of thieves; murdered her best friend under coercion (brother Van held hostage, fingers in an envelope). Now she travels with the woman she killed. Arc: atonement without absolution; Diep's trust is not restored in Book 1, but working respect is. Thread: Van is IN Atlantis. Nam Cam's pit operation is a multiverse harvesting front (the homeless man's line, "I've been waiting months to be chosen," is the seed, already canon from the draft). Mei's search for Van is the bridge to Book 2. Her Rogue kit (Tam's Gobyscale set, the Four Immortals material) stays; it's some of the draft's best work.

### 7.5 Hue Suong, 92 — Priestess of Adonai, the Scene-Stealer
War survivor (field medicine during the American War, canon), world traveler, restored to a two-meter twenty-something body with a morningstar and nine decades of zero patience. **Canon (Tony, from Vovinam's real history):** born ~1932 in Hanoi; as a girl she was among the children in Nguyễn Lộc's earliest Vovinam classes, the art founded under French occupation to give a colonized people a fighting system that was their own and a generation capable of resistance. Her "keep them Vietnamese" is that doctrine, received from the source. She came south with the 1954 migration; keep her later-war family history (draft canon: field medicine, family in the southern resistance) deliberately loose in detail. **Series resonance (locked):** Diep's Founder class is the direct descendant of Lộc's project, a martial School built so a threatened people need not borrow their defense, and Miss Hue is the living link between the two foundings. When Diep's School is founded on Atlantis, Hue is the one who names the lineage. **Dramatization debt:** her founding-generation story is planted in one clause in Ch 1 and must be told properly in a later scene, ideally at the School's founding or the Compact; never as summary. The humans' comedic center of gravity and moral north. Rules for writing her: never a sweet-old-lady joke; the comedy is that she is the most dangerous and least impressed person in every room. She has watched empires lie before, and she has outlived two of them. Watching her re-experience a young body (and grieve the husband her long life outlived) is the quiet B-story. Under the Wall's Ledger (4.6, entry 4) that B-story sharpens: her husband died roughly thirty years ago and may still be waiting in the Arch queue Adonai's wall created. Her patron owes her personally, and she may already suspect it: in Ch 3 she rises to search the faces in the line and sits back down without a word.

### 7.6 Duy Duong, 25 — the Cost (dies Chapter 7, permanent)
Cuong's partner, orphan, the gregarious one. The Ratkin massacre breaks him; his death saving the party at the guardhouse is his choice of repayment. Write him vivid and funny in his six chapters. His death must hurt on release day and still hurt in Book 5. His name becomes party shorthand: "What would Duy carry?" No resurrection. Ever. If readers beg, we're doing it right.

### 7.7 The Gods

### 7.8 Tuan, 16 — the Pain Eater (cold open, Book 1; joins the cast in the Landfall arc)

**Mechanism (canon):** As Adonai's wall decayed, Sakti leaked into Earth across roughly the final two decades before failure. Sensitives worldwide absorbed unshielded exposure: real neurological damage AND real proto-System perception, both from the same leak. Attribution is locked: the harm traces to Adonai's failing wall (the cold open's "He is sorry" is personally addressed to Tuan on reread); the System's sin is lateness, and it apologizes for the delay in documentation, never for the harm, preserving the Auditor's character. Worldwide seed: institutions everywhere hold misdiagnosed sensitives, and at Landfall the backdated-achievement cascade hits all of them at once (Landfall-arc engine; possible pit-network link to sensitive harvesting is an open thread for Book 2 planning, not locked).

**Life (anchors):** First visions in childhood. First hospitalization at 9. The failed report at 11. The war on his abusers, 12 to 14. Discharge at 14, purchased with the spotless record his silence built. Present at the Phu Nhuan massacre at 15, recognizing faces from years-old visions and finding his doctrine useless at the speed of gunfire; readmitted after. Released, sent to the fishing boat of his uncle, Captain Phan Van Loi, out of the Vung Tau coast. 16 at Landfall. **Belief chronology (locked):** as a child he genuinely did not know what the seeings were and half-believed the diagnosis; the Mười years ended the doubt, because a two-year winning book is empirical proof. By 16 he knows the seeings are real. "I don't know" therefore began as truth in childhood and hardened into protective armor; the cold open renders this evolution ("somewhere in the years of saying it, it had quietly become a lie"). Never write him as lifelong-certain or lifelong-doubting; the hinge is the ward book.

**Canon (cold open):** among his seeings, one recurred from childhood, an impossible coastline he drew compulsively on intake forms and worksheets; the doctors read the drawings as fixation. Chronic insomnia is canon (he has rarely ever slept well), which is why he is awake in the bow at dawn, not at the helm; his uncle keeps his pills locked in the cabin and would never trust him with the wheel. At Landfall he recognizes the horizon peak by peak. His first reaction is joy, the first vindication of his life: an impossible thing another person can finally see. His cover lie, "I don't know," is the oldest lie he owns, and Landfall is the first time it weighs nothing. The First Witness achievement reads "You were awake. You always are."

**What was done to him (canon; depiction rules below are absolute):** A diagnosed, disbelieved, medicated child in a state institution is maximally vulnerable, because his testimony has no value, and predators understand what that means. During the first stint, beginning around age 11, a staff orderly abused him, physically and sexually. He reported it once. The report was logged as delusional ideation and his dosage was increased. He never asked to be rescued again. What he did instead is the point.

**The war (canon, with the handler):** The failed report taught him rescue was never coming. The next lesson had a teacher. **Mười Bạc** ("Gambler Ten"; Tuan calls him Chú Mười) arrived on the ward when Tuan was 12: a bookmaker gaming a court-ordered evaluation to dodge sentencing, a sane man hiding among the disbelieved, the inversion of Tuan. He noticed the boy's "ravings" kept coming true, tested him quietly, and opened a book on him: lô đề numbers, match scores, discharge dates, whether it would rain on inspection day, run through a smuggled phone and a runner cousin outside. (Localization lock: no Kalshi or Western prediction markets; the underground lô đề and bookie economy is the authentic mechanism.) The trade was never fair: precision cost Tuan pain, and Mười learned to ration the boy's medication like a handler, sharpening him for query days and soothing him after. But Mười also taught him everything: how staff think, what gets a man fired versus ignored, how to trade, how to be small, how to read an institution the way a bookmaker reads a fixed match. The Arranger campaign was their first joint operation, Tuan supplying the futures and Mười the institutional physics, and abuser number one left in a police car, convicted for the theft, never for the truth. First doctrine, taught and learned: you don't need to be believed to be lethal, you need other people to be believable. The second predator was careful and stole nothing, and one evening Tuan foresaw Mười pushing him down the north stairwell, and said nothing, and watched it arrive, and then told Mười exactly where to be and what to be doing when the questions came. The man lived, wheeled out on disability. The investigation found nothing, because the boy who could see the investigation had edited it in advance. That night cemented them. Mười was released when Tuan was 13 and kept the book running by phone; after Tuan's discharge at 14, Mười's network supplied work, cover, and protection. The family's price for retrieving him after the second hospitalization was the uncle's boat, far from Mười's orbit, and Mười allowed it the way a man allows a deposit to sit in a bank.

**Why Mười matters later (locked as future thread):** at Landfall, somewhere in the HCMC underworld is a bookmaker holding years of winning receipts that prove a true seer exists, in a world that just acquired a System. He will come looking for his oracle. His relationship to Nam Cam's network is open; decide in Book 2 planning.

**THE UNHEARD DOCKET (Tuan's office; locked):** Unique-grade titles carry offices, not just buffs, and First Witness plus Logged Anyway qualify Tuan as court-appointed counsel for the disbelieved. On the court's schedule ("the Docket calls when it calls; scheduling is the court's"), he receives an appointment: a being somewhere in the multiverse who is telling the truth and is surrounded by people who think they are broken. The System knows they are sane, because it believes the record over the reviewer, but belief is outside its jurisdiction, so it appoints advocates from the registry of the vindicated. Sessions convene in vision-space during sleep, which for a chronic insomniac means his only reliable rest is literally court-appointed. Helping pays on a fee schedule scaled to significance: stat grants, Anguish rebates (feeding the Pain Eater track), favors banked, and precedent fame, since his name then appears as "counsel of record" in other beings' award citations across the multiverse. The summons text is canon: "Appointment: Counsel of Record. The following being is telling the truth. No one in their life believes them. You are qualified. Fee schedule attached. Declining is permitted. Counsel was appointed to your case once. Counsel declined." When Tuan accepts, the System answers with its standard line: "Your choice has been logged." Sample clients (seeds, not locks): a young star-whale who keeps insisting the ocean ends (its pod calls it sick; it is approaching the world-rim); a goblin accountant who alone believes the ledgers are haunted (they are); a god's herald who reported that the god is dead (nobody believes him; the god is dead, and the prayers are being answered by an automation). His first advocate was Mười, who believed him for profit; the Docket makes Tuan the Mười who does not take a cut. **Usage cap (binding): at most one Docket case per arc, and every case must braid into the main plot; the Docket is a thematic engine, never episodic filler.**

**The record:** the account "records your body, your deeds, and your debts." At Landfall, Tuan's backdated cascade includes entries he never spoke aloud, with dates, and with a name. The boy nobody believed holds the multiverse's notarized record. The cold open line "Objections: logged anyway" is, on reread, his line. The first abuser was convicted, but for the theft, never the truth; the true entry exists only in the cascade. What he does with the record, testimony rather than revenge, is a Landfall-arc decision (open; names withheld until then).

**Manifestation (the Sarah Connor principle, per Cameron: not crazy, but changed by what he experienced):** at 16 he sleeps with his back to walls, hoards food, reads every room for its exits and its levers, and distrusts kindness because kindness was always an opening move. He is quiet the way a drawn bow is quiet. The history reaches the page as vigilance and leverage, never as cowering.

**The two sins (revised, locked):** His sins are the moral costs of a sane child's survival war, never violent psychosis; the crazy-equals-violent conflation remains banned.
1. **Selection (age 13).** The stairwell. He foresaw the push, chose the future where it happened by saying nothing, and then taught the pusher how to vanish from the record. Not the act, and not even relief: the discovery that editing outcomes worked, felt clean, and left nothing even a god could argue with, and that part of him has been looking for a reason to do it again ever since. The appetite is the sin.
2. **The Quiet (age 14).** His doctrine ran on disciplined silence, aimed at enemies, and then it caught a friendly: he saw a ward-mate's death date and his discipline held. She died on schedule, alone, and the spotless record his silence built became his discharge. The weapon he forged for predators fired through an innocent, and he walked free on the recoil.

**Title chain (backdated cascade processed at Landfall; each entry pays concrete rewards per screen discipline rule 7):** The Warned (childhood, first true vision) → Unheeded ("Awarded for delivering a true warning at personal cost. It did not help. They rarely do.") → **Logged Anyway** (the failed report: "You reported it once. It is the System's policy to believe the record over the reviewer. Entry retained. Name retained.") → **House Odds** (the ward book: "You were the edge. He was the book. The System declines to state which of you profited more.") → **The Arranger** (the fired-and-arrested abuser: "You could not be believed, so you made other people believable. The System notes the efficiency.") → **Selection** (the stairwell: "You never touched him. You chose the future where another man did, then edited the aftermath. The System keeps better records.") → Keeper of the Quiet (the silence that caught a friendly) → Witness of Phu Nhuan (the massacre; the title that binds him to the party by name) → First Witness (Landfall, canon in the cold open). Class track: Prophet, evolving at an accumulated-suffering threshold into **Pain Eater**.

**Pain Eater kit:** Resource: **Anguish**, banked from pain endured, witnessed, or absorbed. Core ability: **Take**, drawing the pain (never the damage) out of another person and holding it; allies fight on wounded and feeling nothing, which is support power and mortal danger in one mechanic. Anguish funds his foresight: precision costs pain, his own or carried. The closed loop is the tragedy: eat pain to fund visions, visions bring more grief. Eating pain is never healing trauma; he is a reservoir, and the series respects what reservoirs eventually do.

**Guardrails (binding, from the pathologized-seer research):** Both the visions and the damage are real. Medication was suppression AND survival, never simply villainous. Vindication is partial, late, and reintroduces burden: the backdated cascade enrages as much as it heals, and off the meds the visions return in force. No tidy absolution: his sins rhyme with the party's massacre, and that rhyme, guilt met with guilt, is the glue when he joins them. He is a survivor, not a revenge fantasy: the humanity anchors are the Quiet's collateral, the Phu Nhuan mismatch (a doctrine built for slow institutional war, useless at the speed of gunfire), and his fear of his own appetite for Selection. Whatever-it-takes is scoped to survival, never sadism, and none of it is ever attributed to illness. **Mười is never laundered:** he groomed a drugged child's talent and profited from queries that cost the boy pain, AND he is the only adult who ever treated Tuan as valuable and taught him to survive. Both stay true on the page; nostalgia and indictment travel together. **Depiction rules for the abuse, absolute and series-long:** never depicted in scene, no graphic description anywhere, ever; conveyed only through implication, aftermath, testimony, and System records; never his defining trait, one wound in a ledger of them. This is the research's no-spectacle rule, it is what makes the restrained versions land, and it is also a hard platform requirement (graphic depiction violates Royal Road and Amazon content policy outright).

**Retrofit ban:** Tuan is NOT visible in Chapter 2. He was one bystander among hundreds at the market. The reveal arrives as his testimony in the Landfall arc, never as an inserted detail in the massacre chapter.

## 8. THE BLOOD DEBT (SPINE QUESTLINE)

> **Quest: Blood Debt.**
> 19 lives were taken that owed you nothing.
> Repayment schedule: to be determined by the survivor.
> Interest accrues.

The System converts the massacre into a permanent, uncapped questline adjudicated by Chit-tat-to. He decides what counts as repayment. This does three jobs: (1) fuses the two protagonists structurally, (2) makes the book's moral engine mechanical (pure LitRPG-native storytelling), (3) generates plot forever: every Ratkin settlement defended, every discarded species given standing, pays principal. Series-scale payoff: the debt is never "paid off." Late in the series, Chit-tat-to closes it himself, as a gift, and it should wreck everyone. Do not foreshadow this in text.

## 9. VOICE GUIDE: THE THREE REGISTERS

1. **The Auditor (System):** dry, precise, procedurally cruel, involuntarily humane. Section 6 rules. Carries page-level comedy so the prose doesn't have to reach for jokes.
2. **Griffin register (human chapters):** competent professionals being understated about horrifying things, rendered the way Griffin actually writes: procedure EXPLAINED with patient, dry precision, never clipped into insider shorthand. Griffin's hallmark is walking the reader through the stance, the weapon, the protocol ("he assumed a boxer's firing stance, one foot back, both hands on the Beretta" is the mode; "front sight, press" is not). The explanation itself carries the authority; clarity IS the register, so jargon is never exempt from self-glossing. Humor from procedure, rank friction, and gallows economy. Banter is earned by competence. The Champion/Companion rank inversion between the siblings is this register's renewable fuel. Correction logged from Tony's catch: earlier drafts conflated Griffin with clipped-jargon thriller shorthand; that conflation is banned.
3. **Brooks register (Chit-tat-to chapters):** warmth, wonder, fable-cadence, big-hearted found-family adventure, with grief underneath. His chapters may be gentler and slightly shorter. They are the palate cleanser that makes the dark chapters land harder.

**Hard bans (Tony's voice rules + genre-punished patterns):** no em/en dashes; no rhetorical questions in narration; no "not X but Y" constructions; no dramatic colons; minimal adjectives; no character introduced via height/weight stats (physicality through action only); no reference-joke humor; no head-hopping (POV changes at chapter breaks only); no lore as documents; no repeated notification spam.

**Craft rules added from Tony's Ch 1-4 notes (apply to every chapter going forward):**
1. **First-line rule.** Every chapter, and every major POV or scene shift inside a chapter, opens on motion, sensation, or threat. Never a wry framing observation. Applies with maximum force to the first line of the book and the first line of every book. Test: a tired reader on a phone keeps scrolling.
2. **Banter clarity.** A joke must land on first read. If the gag requires re-reading the previous line to decode, rewrite it explicit or cut it.
3. **No unearned weight, no image-riddles.** A line only gets gravitas the scene has already banked. If a sentence is reaching for depth (the "sidearm and newspapers" failure), it either gets set up properly or it dies. Same for sensory observations: if the image requires decoding ("Distance should have made it soft. It was not soft."), replace it with the concrete version of what it was reaching for ("Land that far away should have been a blue smudge on the sky. Tuan could count the peaks.") or cut it. When in doubt, go lighter and more literal.
4. **Miss Hue is the calibration reference.** Her Ch 1-4 register (dry authority, patience as a weapon, jokes that are also verdicts) is confirmed correct. When unsure how a scene should sound, ask what Miss Hue would do to it.
5. **Read-aloud check.** Every chapter gets a repetition and cadence pass as if narrated (this book is audio-first). Doubled words in close proximity, stacked trailing clauses, and passive constructions get caught here.
6. **Vietnamese term convention.** Character and place names romanize without diacritics for reader ease (Diep, Cuong, Phu Nhuan, Bien Dong). Cultural terms italicize with full diacritics on first use and carry an organic gloss (*tất niên*, "the party that sees the old year out before Tết"; *lô đề*). Glosses read as apposition a Vietnamese reader would not find condescending, never as encyclopedia entries.
7. **Dramatize, never report.** Cool content is never summarized ("she had hunted elk with a bow in Mongolia, and she was not above mentioning it" is the banned form). If a story, feat, or history is worth the reader's time, it goes on the page as scene: dialogue, action, texture. Oft-told family stories are delivered as call-and-response, characters completing each other's sentences and correcting details, which shows intimacy while the content lands. If it is not worth dramatizing, cut it entirely. There is no middle register.

**Canonized in the cold open (lore now locked):** worlds customarily receive one century of notice before integration; Earth's century was spent hidden by Adonai's decision, and the System says so publicly ("The decision has been logged. He is sorry."). Adonai's culpability is on the record from page one, unnamed until Chapter 3.

**Per research:** the dry-military register is the least-proven bet in the genre. Validation gate: chapters 1-10 go to beta readers before we write past chapter 20. If the Griffin register reads as low-energy, we warm the human chapters toward DCC-standard banter and keep Griffin concentrated in Cuong.

## 10. BOOK 1 STRUCTURE — 60 CHAPTERS, ~150,000 WORDS

Target: ~2,500 words/chapter. Hook lands Chapter 4. Chit-tat-to POV begins Chapter 9 (early enough to establish the dual structure inside the RR trial window). POV rhythm from Act 2 onward: roughly two Diep chapters per Chit-tat-to chapter, flexing for arc needs. Every chapter ends on a pull (question, threat, or cost).

**Cold open (pre-chapter, one page):** Integration Day, one year from now. A fishing crew in the South China Sea watches the horizon grow a coastline. No named characters, no stat blocks, one System line to close: "**World Event: Landfall.** Estimated time to impact: you have already run out."

### ACT 2 — ATLANTIS, THE DEBT, THE ROAD (Ch. 13-40)
- **Ch 13. Landfall (Small).** The party arrives at the human wedge's frontier, near the Goblinkind border. First sight of Atlantis as lived-in place, not lore. Rank friction: the System refuses Cuong's first order.
- **Ch 14. CHIT-TAT-TO II: The Village of Nineteen Absences.** Funeral rites in pictograms. His mother's absence. The elders want to move the village deeper into hiding, forever. He wants the debt paid. He leaves.
- **Ch 15. The Frontier Town.** Leath's edge: refugee politics, human faction map through bar-room texture. Rumor of prior champions who vanished. First paying quest.
- **Ch 16. First Blood, Properly.** A contract hunt goes sideways; the party wins ugly using Earth skills + System mechanics in combination. Leveling montage done through consequence, not numbers.
- **Ch 17. CHIT-TAT-TO III: The Gremlin.** He recruits (or is adopted by) his first companion: a gremlin exile with a broken oath and a genius for traps. Two discarded creatures form a party of two. The System issues them a party name it appears to have chosen with care.
- **Ch 18. The Wall.** Echo of the cut prologue, repurposed: the party serves a rotation on a contested border wall and sees what 500 years of war means. Gnomish infiltrators. Diep's first command under fire.
- **Ch 19. The Price of Ammunition.** A fight won by the last full magazine. The guns' decline made strategic and felt. Cuong begins training the party in Vovinam-System hybrid drills.
- **Ch 20. CHIT-TAT-TO IV: Words.** His sentience stat crosses a threshold. A full sentence renders. What it says, and what he does about it, is the act's emotional midpoint. (Design target: this chapter is the one readers screenshot.)
- **Ch 21-23. The Hobgoblin Road.** Three-chapter arc: the party crosses contested territory, encounters an oath-broken hobgoblin veteran (future Chit-tat-to recruit; plant here), and learns Goblinkind is not a monolith. Mei hears the word "pits" in a context that stops her cold.
- **Ch 24. CHIT-TAT-TO V: The Invoice.** The co-leads reunite. Chit-tat-to, through the gremlin's translation and his own growing words, delivers terms: the debt will be repaid on his schedule, in his currency. The party accepts. The two stories fuse.
- **Ch 25-27. The Settlement Arc.** First Blood Debt payment: defend/relocate the Ratkin village against a raider threat the humans' own escape provoked (consequence chain from Act 1). Diep commands allied nonhumans for the first time. Hue and the Ratkin elders scene (two survivors of long wars recognize each other).
- **Ch 28. CHIT-TAT-TO VI: A Name on a Map.** The village, defended, is chartered in the System as a named settlement, the first Ratkin place-name in Atlantis's ledger. His screen renders it in words AND pictograms. His people can read it too.
- **Ch 29-31. Leath.** The capital arc: claim politics from the inside, the Partition explained through a parliamentary fight over proxy assignments (never as a document), the missing-champions mystery deepens (the Burundi/Malawi group surfaces, changed). The announcement: Vietnam's claim has been formally contested, adjudication scheduled this wave. Patron scene: the Four Immortals, favor spent and earned.
- **Ch 32. Mei's Thread.** Evidence Van is alive: the pit network supplies forced proxies for claim contests. Someone is fielding enslaved Earth fighters to take Earth territory. Seed only; detonates in Book 2.
- **Ch 33. CHIT-TAT-TO VII: The Recruit.** The hobgoblin veteran and the stranded Kilar join. Extra charge under the Partition: the hobgoblin's species HAS claim standing, and he walks away from it to follow a Ratkin. Their first quest as a unit, warm and fable-bright against the gathering dark.
- **Ch 34-36. The Standing Gauntlet.** Vietnam's defense standing must be confirmed against rival claimants and Leath politicking. Leath extracts a promise: the party will stand proxy for a claim of Leath's choosing, a debt that will hurt later. The sibling duo fights as one unit in public for the first time since childhood tournaments. Diep confirmed as Champion of standing.
- **Ch 37. The Auditor's Aside.** Quiet chapter before the storm. Wills written (Griffin register at full strength), inventory, a System message that is almost, deniably, kind. Chit-tat-to declares his party will enter the contest at Vietnam's side. The law says discarded races have no standing. He says the law is about to be wrong.
- **Ch 38-40. The No-Man's Land.** The crossing to the Adjudication Grounds: 8 million square kilometers of the multiverse's nightmares. One major loss-scare (no death; spend the tension permadeath has earned), first contact with a rival world's champion cohort. Ends: the Grounds fill the horizon, and above them, the world map burns grey and red.

### ACT 3 — THE VIETNAM CLAIM (Ch. 41-60)
- **Ch 41. The Grounds.** Arrival. The world map as scoreboard: most of Earth grey (undefended), too much already red. Contest rules delivered by the Auditor in its finest hour.
- **Ch 42. Watching a Loss.** Another nation's contest, spectated phase by phase to the end. The claim turns red; the reinforced horde assignment posts publicly. The party (and the reader) learns contest grammar by watching a team fail.
- **Ch 43. No Standing.** Roster crisis: Chit-tat-to's party is denied entry. The block comes from a claims-broker consortium that profits from the discarded staying discarded, and it has lawyers. Villain faction introduced through paperwork, which is scarier.
- **Ch 44. CHIT-TAT-TO VIII: The Petition.** His appeal, argued in pictograms and his growing words, logged by the Auditor with unusual thoroughness. Denied. For now. The denial contains, against policy, instructions for refiling.
- **Ch 45-46. The Sixty Hours.** Prep: phase briefings, the School's first mass training session (Founder mechanics on full display), the attrition math of a three-phase contest laid out like an operations order. Ends: the Phase 1 gate opens on a rendered Mekong delta.
- **Ch 47-49. Phase One: The Delta.** Capture-and-hold across flooded terrain with a drifting objective (Ring Space adaptation). The enemy fields disciplined mercenary companies. First entries on the attrition ledger: resources spent, wounds that will carry, one allied NPC death that stays dead. Won, at cost.
- **Ch 50-51. Phase Two: The Highlands.** Siege and escort. The ledger bites: everything spent in the delta is still spent. Mei identifies pit-proxies among the enemy: enslaved fighters, some of them human. The moral floor drops out.
- **Ch 52. CHIT-TAT-TO IX: Standing.** Mid-contest, the refiled petition lands. The Compact is sealed on the field: not fealty, a treaty between Diep's School and Chit-tat-to's party as equals, and the Grounds recognize it. The first Ratkin in history enters a claim contest as a combatant of standing. His screen renders the word. His people, watching the broadcast at the village, see it rendered in pictograms.
- **Ch 53-54. Phase Two Falls.** The highlands are lost: the book's first lost phase. The contest continues, but Phase 3 begins with the enemy holding the ledger advantage. Diep's command decisions under real weight; Cuong's shield work buys the retreat; the rank inversion stops being funny.
- **Ch 55-58. Phase Three: Phu Nhuan.** The final phase renders the market where they died. The broker's champion anchors the enemy line (boss design on the Broken Clock timing framework). Pit-proxies fighting in the streets they grew up on. Duy's absence in the formation, felt, not stated. Diep spends something irreplaceable: her School's founding boon, held since her Trial, used not for power but to make the Compact's standing permanent beyond this contest. The Blood Debt and the mission price against each other for the first time, and she pays. The market holds.
- **Ch 59. The Ledger.** Vietnam turns green on the world map. Integration terms written in humanity's favor, itemized by the Auditor like a settlement document. The Blood Debt ticks down on-screen against three specific names. Chit-tat-to closes the chapter at the history wall, recording all of it, in fluent words.
- **Ch 60. Interest.** Triple hook-out: (1) the missing Italians surface, standing proxy for claims no one assigned them, and something is wrong with how they fight; (2) a broadcast reaches Mei: Van, alive, fighting in the pits, and someone on Atlantis is betting on him; (3) the Auditor, unprompted, issues the party a quest it should not have the authority to issue. End of Book 1.

## 11. SERIES MAP (SKETCH, FLEXIBLE)

**11.5 POWER CURVE DOCTRINE (locked, from Tony's design; System Universe as model and cautionary tale):** Cuong goes deep in the hole at Ch 5 (severe garnishment, exact fraction set in drafting, plus Debtor visibility) and climbs as a badass through Book 1 into early Book 2: the fighting genius at level nothing, winning on skill while the numbers starve. **The Escrow:** garnished XP is held, not destroyed; the reader watches his true level accrue in a locked account; full satisfaction at the end of Book 2 releases principal plus restoration interest, detonating him and the party into OP mode with every point pre-witnessed. Face-slapping is ledgered: every door closed on the Debtor is on record and reverses at satisfaction. **Compensation for OP (the SunriseCV lessons):** the cast carries challenge via staggered curves, someone is always mid-climb (Chit-tat-to and Tuan peak Book 3+, Miss Hue holds burst divinity, Diep's Founder class is OP as force multiplier, which raises stakes instead of trivializing them). **The jurisdictional ceiling:** endgame conflicts are legal and political (the Partition's rule-bound brackets, the claims map, the Wall's Ledger, the Authors), which no stat total shortcuts; power makes the party bigger litigants, never finished ones. The documented SU failure mode, an OP lead with nothing to do, is structurally excluded: the court always has another docket.
- **Book 2:** Contest seasons + the pits + Van; the harvesting network exposed; Chit-tat-to's settlement becomes a movement.
- **Book 3:** The missing-champions conspiracy; what the Adjudication Grounds actually are; first major faction alliance won.
- **Books 4-6:** Integration approaches; Atlantis politics at scale; the System's conscience mystery matures.
- **Integration Day** (the cold open, arrived at last) is a mid-series event, not the finale. The series' back half is Earth + Atlantis.

**PLANTED PAYOFFS (promises made on the page; must be kept):**
- **Tuan, the fisherboy.** Cold open, Book 1. Full character design in Section 7.8. Returns in the Landfall arc; do not reference him again before then; the silence is the hook.
- **Miss Hue's search of the Arch queue** (Ch 3). She rises to scan the faces in the line and sits back down without a word. Her husband, dead ~30 years, may still be in that queue (Wall's Ledger entry 4). Pays off when her faith in Adonai and his personal debt to her collide; timing open, but the promise is now on the page and must be kept.
- **Who declined Tuan's file.** The summons states the fact plainly ("Counsel was appointed to your case once. Counsel declined.") and volunteers nothing more. When Tuan accepts his first appointment, the System answers, "Your choice has been logged." The detonation is delayed by design: in a later book, Tuan recalls that exchange and completes the syllogism himself, his choice was logged, therefore the decliner's choice was logged, therefore a name exists in the record, therefore there is someone to find. The realization must be his deduction, never a System disclosure. Do not answer, or let him assemble it, in Book 1.
- **Chị Ba (Miss Hue's war name).** Named on the page in Ch 1: the old men at the Vovinam school call her Chị Ba, Sister Three, and when nine-year-old Diep asked why, every one of them found somewhere else to be. The NAME is public; the REASON is the payoff. Candidate (strong, not locked): the System already knows, and her Atlantis title or class designation states what Chị Ba means, so the multiverse tells the reader what Vietnam never would. Do not explain it in narration before then.
- **Miss Hue and the founding of Vovinam** (Ch 1, one clause: "one of the children in his earliest classes"). Her full story of the art born under occupation must be dramatized in a later scene, at Diep's School founding or the Compact, where she names Diep's Founder class as the lineage's next chapter. Never delivered as summary.
- **Mười Bạc's receipts.** Years of winning bets prove a true seer exists, held by an underworld bookmaker in HCMC. At Landfall he comes looking for his oracle. Must pay off in the Landfall arc.
- **The Auditor's unauthorized quest** (Ch 60). Book 2 opens on it.
- **Duy's name** as party shorthand ("What would Duy carry?"). Recurs series-long.
- **The Blood Debt closure** (Chit-tat-to's gift, late series). Never foreshadowed in text.
