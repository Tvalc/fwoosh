# BLOOD DEBT — FOUR-AUTHOR CRAFT REVIEW
## Every issue caught to date, sorted by which author's lens catches it, then applied chapter by chapter.

---

## PART A: THE ISSUE TAXONOMY, BY LENS

### SANDERSON catches structure and promises
His tools: promise/progress/payoff; First Law (a resolution satisfies in proportion to how well the reader understands the rules that produce it); Second Law (limitations are more interesting than powers); Third Law (expand what exists before adding new); scene/sequel rhythm (action, then reaction-dilemma-decision); legible motivation; invisible prose; chapter-end hooks that deliver new information.
Issues he caught in our book:
1. Resolutions that ignored established rules (Ch16 v1: monster leaves on its own; fixed by Diep weaponizing Steady Hands).
2. Missing sequels: violence without reaction beats (Ch9 v1 jumped burial to raven; Ch3 v1 jumped waking to beating).
3. Plans executed that were never shown (Ch7: "the way Cuong had built it in the dark" with no plan on the page).
4. Payoffs without setup (Ch7's "19 plus 5" transfer with no prior Duy judgment; the Founder allotment raised twice, never pressed).
5. A five-chapter tension sag (9 through 13) between the gate and the terraces.
6. Supporting cast without wants (Duy, fixed with Da Lat).
7. Withholding as shorthand: coy phrasing that hides facts the reader already owns ("somebody had made a decision about that").

### DINNIMAN catches the System and the tone
His tools: the System as a character with a voice; notifications as jokes AND dread; comedy timing inside horror; tonal whiplash as a feature; every screen does character work; momentum; stakes stated in numbers the reader tracks.
Issues he caught:
1. Screens that arrived without event-ness (fixed by the judgment-and-precedent register).
2. A second voice that would have cloned his announcer (fixed: court, not game show).
3. Screens that inform without characterizing (all rewritten to rule, editorialize, or withhold).
4. Death screens that decorated (silent-screen doctrine).
5. Numbers the reader cannot follow (garnishment now shows its math every time).

### BROOKS catches earnestness and the co-lead
His tools: wonder without irony; landscape as mood; fellowship as engine; measured cadence; ordinary protagonists; clear moral stakes.
Issues he caught:
1. Chit-tat-to absent for thirteen chapters after the massacre that defines him (fixed: Ch14 and Ch17).
2. The Ratkin faceless until Ch6 (fixed: the row, the comb, the red cord).
3. Atlantis arriving as lore instead of place (fixed: Ch8 terrace, Ch13 town).
4. Emotion narrated instead of enacted in the Brooks chapters ("something in his chest came apart").

### GRIFFIN catches procedure and register
His tools: procedure explained at point of use; institutional detail exact; dialogue-heavy; understatement; short paragraphs and one-fact-per-line inside exchanges; deadpan official humor; NEVER insider shorthand.
Issues he caught (the largest category):
1. Clipped jargon mistaken for his voice (blade of the body, front sight press, edge-on, controlled pairs, cordite, steaming, string bag, farrier, reach).
2. Telegraphese ("Assessment, automatic").
3. Walls of text in firefights (fixed: one fact per paragraph).
4. Unexplained competence (rookie as SWAT; explained via the District 8 shootout).
5. Wounds without physiology (arm dead below the elbow from a shoulder hit; one-handed without cause; ghost injuries after restoration).
6. Deaths without mechanism (Cuong dying of a shoulder wound; fixed with two rounds shown).
7. Missing partner talk (fixed: Cuong and Duy across the lane).
8. Passive "there was/were" in action.

### THE FOUR RULES THAT SIT ABOVE ALL FOUR LENSES (Tony's laws)
- THE FINAL SENTENCE LAW: the last sentence of a paragraph is a fact or it dies.
- NO NARRATED INTERIORITY: bodies, not explanations of minds.
- NOBODY HARDENED BEFORE THE PAGE HARDENS THEM.
- WRITE THE WORDS PEOPLE SAY; never summarize speech in high emotion.

## PART C: STANDING ORDERS GOING FORWARD
1. Every new chapter gets the four-lens read before delivery: Sanderson (is the resolution built from known rules; is there a sequel; what's promised), Dinniman (does every screen characterize), Brooks (is wonder earned and enacted), Griffin (is every procedure explained and every wound physical).
2. Word count is an output, never a target. Short chapters that serve pacing (2, 4) stay short. Thin chapters (5-8, 10-13) grow by sequels and rules-in-scene, never by description.
3. Tuan must be re-touched by Ch20.
4. The Founder allotment and Mei's fine print must be pressed by Ch19.
