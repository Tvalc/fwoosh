## CHAPTER 11: THE ASSAY

The hall at Threshold took the sound out of everything. The door that closed behind Diep was not the door she had walked through.

One moment the four of them stood in the classing hall under ceilings built for wings, with Odin talking to a clerk-shaped column of light, and the next Diep stood alone on a wooden floor she knew before she understood knowing it. Phan Dang Luu. The training hall. Evening light through the high windows, the smell of mats and liniment, every board of it correct, and the writing hanging where the master's portrait should have been.

> Assay: commenced.
> This is a simulacrum. Nothing here persists. Everything here counts.

The far door of the training hall stood barred, and through its slats she could see fire, and between her and the door were nine people she had never met, civilians, market-clothes, terror on them like sweat, and the writing continued, patient as a proctor.

> The bar lifts only under a two-person guard technique, executed correctly, simultaneously, at both brackets.
> You know the technique.
> None of them do.
> The fire does not care. You have what time you have.

Diep looked at her own hands. Twelve years, and every hour of it spent making her body better, and not one hour spent putting what her body knew into someone else's, and the System had read her to the floorboards, because that was exactly the shape of the hole in her.

She picked the calmest-looking one, a wiry aunty with flour on her sleeves.

"You," Diep said, and heard her own voice shake, and kept going anyway, the way you walk out when the bracket posts. "Look at me, not the fire. Feet like this."

She kicked off her shoes, red canvas, the good ones, because you do not step on a mat shod, her body did it before her mind voted, and the writing flickered small in the corner of her eye.

> Cultural rite recognized: unshod instruction.
> Honest Feet: minor bonus applied.
> The Authors held that the honest walk barefoot. The System declines to explain further.

She had no time to read it twice.

She taught badly, then less badly. Hands on the aunty's wrists. Breathe. Again. Lower. Again. Good. She used the master's old cadence because it was the only teaching voice she had.

The fire came through the far side of the door.

On the ninth attempt the aunty's bracket turned with hers, together, and the bar came up.

Nine strangers who did not exist went through into a cool dark that did not exist either. Diep stood alone in her childhood, barefoot, shaking.

The hall faded. The writing stayed.

> Assay: concluded.
> You were offered strength and chose transmission. This has been the pattern of your line for one hundred years. It is now the pattern of your record.

> **Class offered: THE FOUNDER.**
> Grade: Unique. One per world.
> Precedent: this class has issued on 61 worlds.
> Most recent prior recipient: Ashkava of Drenn, subsequently First Teacher of the Nine Free Cities.
> Outcomes are not guaranteed.
> Mechanic: TRANSMISSION. You may co-author classes for those you train, one rank above your own attainment. Their oaths bind to your School. Their deeds enter your ledger, credits and debts alike.
> Constraint: School-bound. A Founder who abandons her School is reclassified. The word for the reclassification is not shown until relevant.
> Accept?

Diep read it three times. *Their deeds enter your ledger, credits and debts alike.* Somewhere outside the simulacrum, her brother stood under nineteen and five and eleven findings, and she had already signed for him once.

"Accept," she said, and the training hall let her go.
