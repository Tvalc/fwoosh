## COLD OPEN: LANDFALL

*One year from now.*

The horizon had grown a coastline.

There were no fish in the Bien Dong that night, the wide sea the world's maps call the South China. The nets had come up empty three times, and toward midnight Captain Phan Van Loi had pointed the bow at better water and told his crew to sleep, because the diesel was burning either way.

A crewman held the wheel on the straight heading, an old man with a face like a dried fig, steering with his chin on his chest.

Tuan, sixteen, the captain's nephew, taken aboard as a family favor nobody discussed, his pills kept in a locked drawer in the captain's cabin, was awake in the bow because he could not sleep. He had rarely ever been able to. He was a thin boy, small for sixteen, five years of institution food and a year of ship work in him, with arms that had learned to haul nets.

That is how the least important man on the boat became the first to see it.

He did not wake the captain. The pills made the things he saw small and far away, like weather on another coast, and he waited for this one to fade the way the others always faded. It did not fade. He would say later that he had spent five years learning what happens to a boy who reports things that cannot be there, and that he had wanted to be very sure.

The coastline ran north and south past both edges of the world, gray at the waterline, then green, then white at the mountaintops in the first sun. The chart pinned in the wheelhouse said there was no land within two days of this water.

Tuan did not need the chart to know the shape on the horizon. He had been drawing that skyline since he was nine, on intake forms and the backs of worksheets, and the doctors had a word for a boy who draws the same imaginary coast year after year.

There was surf breaking on it now. He could count the peaks, and he knew them the way he knew his own name.

His uncle came up the ladder with his coffee, a squat weathered man who had run this boat for thirty years, and froze with one foot still on the rung.

On the depth sounder, the numbers that told him how much water lay under the boat were falling like a dropped stone. Three hundred meters. Then two hundred. Then ninety.

It meant the sea floor was climbing toward them out of water that had been two kilometers deep on every chart he had ever owned. It meant that if they held this heading they would run aground on a coast that had not been there an hour ago.

Birds streamed overhead, more than either of them had ever seen, all flying the wrong way, out to sea, screaming.

Tuan was not watching the coastline anymore. He was watching his uncle see it. His whole life, no one else had ever seen the things he saw. That was the entire problem with him, according to everyone who had ever been paid to have an opinion. Now one of those things stood in the open ocean with surf breaking on it, his uncle was staring straight at it, and the feeling rising in Tuan's chest was so unfamiliar it took him a breath to recognize it as joy.

"What country is that?" his uncle said, smaller than Tuan had ever heard him.

"I don't know," Tuan said.

It was the coastline from his drawings, peak for peak. He had known it since he was nine. He had said *I don't know* to doctors and to his own mother for so long that it came out on its own.

Then, in front of both their eyes, hanging over the water like the afterimage of a flash, blue-bordered and patient, appeared writing.

> **World Event: Landfall.**
> Your world's exemption expired at dawn, local time. Enrollment: universal. Consent: not required. Objections: logged anyway.
> Worlds customarily receive one century of notice before integration. Yours was spent hidden, by the decision of a third party. The decision has been logged. He is sorry.
> An account has been opened in your name. It records your body, your deeds, and your debts. You may inspect it at any time by thinking the words "status screen."
> You are Level 1. Almost everyone is.
> Estimated time to impact: you have already run out.

And in front of the boy alone, a second message, smaller, addressed.

> **Achievement: First Witness.**
> Of eight billion, you saw it first. You were awake. You always are.
> Grade: Unique. One per world. This will never be awarded again.
> Precedent: this award has issued on 1,406 worlds.
> Most recent prior recipient: Szel of Vekh-Amun, subsequently First Navigator of the Unbroken Fleet.
> Outcomes are not guaranteed.
> Awarded: Wisdom +3. Luck +3.
> Skill recognized: **Horizon Eyes** (Unique). You have had this for some time. The System apologizes for the delay in documentation.
> Backdated entries: processing. There are many.
> The System suggests you remember this day. It will.
