## CHAPTER 5: THE READING

The torch nearest Diep guttered, and in its failing light the writing began.

It came up out of the dark blue-bordered and patient, the way it had come over the water for a fisherman she would never meet, and it arranged itself in front of each of them separately, so that five people stood in one stone room in five private columns of light, being read to.

> Your party has been awarded 700 experience.
> Experience is awarded without regard to circumstance.
> This is not the System's favorite rule either.

Diep read it twice. Behind her, in the dark, the row of small bodies they had dragged to the wall did not move.

> **Title earned: Caedite Eos.**
> Awarded for the elimination of 24 hostiles.
> 19 of the 24 have been reclassified: non-combatant.
> Reclassification does not affect the award. Titles, like history, are written by the survivors.

"Turn it off," Duy said. His voice came from somewhere below his usual voice. "How do you turn it off."

Nobody answered him.

The columns kept unspooling. Diep's own text was short. A skill she had never trained arriving at a number she had never earned, a title she would have paid anything to refuse, and at the bottom, in smaller print, a line that she understood was aimed at her alone.

> Skill increased: Short Blades, Level 21.
> You are now measurably better at this than your brother.
> He has been notified.

She looked at Cuong. He was not looking at her. He stood still inside his own column, and his column was long, and it was still coming.

She crossed to him and read over his shoulder, and that was how she learned what the last six months had been.

> Skill recognized: Combat Firearms, Journeyman.
> Provenance: six months of foreign drills for an assignment you never named to your sister.

> **Title earned: Need to Know.**
> Awarded for 183 days of operational secrecy, maintained against colleagues, criminals, and one persistent little sister.
> Effect: hostile appraisal, interrogation, and mind-reading attempts against you are impaired.
> The System is exempt. The System was always exempt.

"Persistent," Diep said.

"Later," Cuong said quietly, and the word had no later in it, because the column was still coming.

> **Title earned: The Last Hand.**
> You had eight rounds and one hand. The record does not show a decision. The record shows where your hand was.
> The System has no instrument for measuring this. It has ruled on it anyway.
> Effect, Steady Hands: while a wounded ally is within your reach, your aim, your composure, and your tolerance for pain improve considerably.
> Effect, Tunnel: when blood relations take harm in your presence, all other threats leave your perception for a time.
> The court declines to call the second one a penalty. The court records it as consistent.

> **JUDGMENT: BLOOD DEBT.**
> Findings: 19 non-combatants, deceased, your instrument.
> Balance: 19.
> Sentence: garnishment. Nine parts in ten of all experience earned is withheld against the balance.
> Withheld experience is not destroyed. It is held.
> Satisfaction in kind: a life preserved at personal risk credits the balance.
> This judgment cannot be dispelled, resisted, prayed away, or outrun.
> It can only be repaid.

> **JUDGMENT: THE ACCOUNTING.**
> Duty recorded: protection of the public.
> Civilians lost within your engagement: 11.
> The System does not apportion fault. It rules on totals.

> **JUDGMENT: FAILURE OF CHARGE.**
> The court recognizes oaths, including the ones never spoken.
> You swore yours when you were six years old, standing at a hospital window.
> She died within reach of your hand.
> Breach recorded.

Cuong read it, and read it again, and then his legs stopped holding him and he sat down in the dirt of the cell floor with his back against the stone and his hand over his mouth. He did not make much noise. That was somehow the worst part of it, Diep would think later, that her brother cried the way a man cries when he is trying not to wake anyone up.

She read the last judgment three times, and on the third reading she stopped being able to see it, and understood she was crying too.

"That isn't fair," she said. "You were shot. You saved me. You saved Mei. None of that is fair."

"It isn't about fair." Miss Hue had come up on Cuong's other side. She read the judgments top to bottom the way she read newspapers, completely, withholding comment until the end. "It's a court. I have seen courts before. Fair is not what they sell."

The final screen arrived for all five of them at once, one shared column in the center of the room.

> Judgments against Companion Cuong Loc have been entered.
> Debtor status: visible on appraisal.
> The court accepts sureties. A surety co-signs the debt, shares the garnishment, and is compensated at satisfaction.
> Who stands surety?

The room held still. Duy was looking at the floor, at the row of small bodies at the edge of the torchlight, at his own hands. Mei stood apart, under a column of her own text that she had angled away from everyone, and did not share it, and did not look up.

Diep's mouth was dry.

"I do," she said.

> Confirm: you accept a share of the garnishment.
> Your growth will be slower. His survival becomes more likely.
> This is the whole of the arrangement. The court does not decorate it.

"Diep." Cuong's voice cracked on her name. "No. You don't sign for me. That's not how, that has never been how we"

"You're not in charge of how," Diep said, and her voice shook, and she signed anyway.

> Surety recorded: Diep Loc, Champion of Vietnam.
> The court notes, for the record, that she signed before she stopped shaking.

Down the corridor, beyond the door none of them had opened, something heavy dragged across stone, and stopped, and dragged again.
