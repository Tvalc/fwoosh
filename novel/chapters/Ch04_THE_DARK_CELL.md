## CHAPTER 4: THE DARK CELL

They fell in darkness for half a heartbeat and hit stone.

Diep landed on her feet, absorbed it, dropped low. The dark was almost total. Four torches, one to a wall, guttering, far apart, and a cold that came up through the floor and smelled of earth and smoke and animals. A big room. She could feel the size of it in how the torch light died before it found the corners.

"Sound off," Cuong said quietly, somewhere left. Cop voice, low and level. "Diep."

"Here."

"Duy."

"Here." Flat. Wrong. She marked it.

"Miss Hue."

"Here."

A pause with a decision in it. "Mei."

"Here." It came out thin and wet, through the broken face.

Cloth moved in the dark. Diep's eyes, adjusting, found her brother's silhouette against the nearest torch, the Beretta already drawn and held low, muzzle at the floor, the way police carry a gun when they have not yet chosen a target. The service belt had come with him. There was weight on her own forearms that had not been there in the market. Her hands found it in the dark: her competition knives, sheathed and strapped where she wore them on the mat. She had died in a romper with nothing on her arms. Adonai had said provisions, and this was what a god thought a girl from Phu Nhuan needed for a dark room on another world, the two blades she had won a trophy with on Sunday.

She put her thumb on the pommel of the left one and held it there.

Then the far side of the room began to whisper.

It was not wind. It was voices, high and quick, dozens of them, a chittering tide rising and falling, and under it the scrape of feet and something else, a dry sliding that her mind refused to place. Shapes detached from the dark across the room, small, the size of children, and more behind them, and more.

"Contact front," Duy breathed. "Twenty plus."

"Nobody fires," Cuong said. "Eyes on them."

Text bloomed in the air, bright in the dark, blue-bordered, blocking half her vision at exactly the wrong moment.

> **You have been offered a quest**

"Down, not now." Diep swatted at it and it obeyed, folding away into a small pulsing dot at the edge of sight. Across the room Duy cursed at his own floating light. Screens hissed out, all of them, five people clearing their eyes at once.

That was the moment the shapes came forward into the torchlight.

They were not children.

Snouts. Teeth in the snouts. Fur in reds and grays, naked tails dragging, eyes catching the torch flame in flat coins of green and yellow. Rags for clothes. In the front rank, blades of crude dark iron, and two long spears, leveled.

The whole mass of them came on with the chittering rising to a scream.

Diep got her hands up, open, empty, and took one step forward, and got as far as "We're not"

"Weapons!" Duy's voice cracked. "They're charging!"

"Hold," Cuong said.

The lead spear carrier shrieked something and broke into a run. Three more came behind it, blades up, twenty meters and closing fast in the dark, straight at her brother.

Cuong fired.

The room turned into a strobe.

Muzzle flash froze the charge in white instants. Shapes mid-stride. Mouths open. The sound in the stone box hit like a fist, over and over, two guns working.

Diep was on the floor with her arms over her head and her ears shrieking. In the stuttering light she saw the small bodies going down and going down, and the ones behind them turning, trying to turn, climbing over each other away from the thunder.

And the guns kept working, because in the dark and the flash and the terror there was no way left to tell a spear from a raised hand, a soldier from a body being carried.

Then the shooting stopped, and the silence was total.

Her ears rang. Smoke and burned powder hung in the torchlight in slow galaxies. Nothing on the far side of the room was chittering anymore.

"Reloading," Duy said, automatic, and then said nothing else.

They crossed the room together, Cuong first, weapon still up. The light found the details one at a time. The spear carriers, yes, two of them, big for their kind, dead at the front. Three with knives near them. And behind and among and beneath them, the others. The small ones smaller than the rest. The gray-furred ones with backs bent by age, dead in a heap where they had been shielding something, and the something was smaller still.

Most of them had been running away from the ones who charged. The room had been dark and the guns had been fast.

Duy made a sound she had never heard a man make and went down onto his knees in the blood, and his hands hovered over one of the small ones without touching it, the way you hover over a thing you might still break.

Miss Hue began, very quietly, to pray. It did not sound like comfort. It sounded like testimony.

Mei walked past all of them.

She went to the nearest of the small bodies with her one working arm, took it under the shoulders, and began dragging it toward the wall, slowly, laying it straight. Then the next. Nobody had told her to. Nobody stopped her. She did not look at any of them while she did it, and by the fourth body Duy had gotten up off his knees and taken the other end.

Diep watched them lay the row and could not make her own feet move.

The dot at the edge of Diep's vision pulsed.

She did not want to open it.

Her brother was standing in the middle of the room with the Beretta hanging from his hand, and he was not looking at the bodies, and he was not looking at the screen, and he was not looking at anything.

Someone had to. She opened it.

The screen unfolded with perfect courtesy.

> **You were offered a quest by Odin.**
> **Save the Ratkin.**
> You have been placed among another party of prisoners, the Ratkin. Establish communication. Help each other escape.
> Difficulty: Hard.
> Reward: allies, freedom, 1,000 experience.
> This offer expired 41 seconds after issue.

> **Quest Failed: Save the Ratkin.**
> Cause of failure: you shot them.
> Favor with Odin: -100.
> This entry is permanent. So are they.
