## CHAPTER 9: THE GOD OF WHAT REMAINS

The raven landed on the terrace rail with a sound like a dropped anvil.

It came apart, and a man stood in its place. Wolf fur. One eye. A spear that grounded on the stone with a crack Cuong felt in his back teeth.

The eye went over the four of them, counted, and stopped where the fifth should have been.

"The loud one," Odin said.

"Duy," Cuong said. "Officer Duy. He held a gate."

"I know what he held. I watched." The eye came to rest on Cuong, and on the judgments over Cuong's head, and it read them without hurry, the way everyone on this world would now read him before they read his face. "You are expensive, policeman. Nineteen and five and eleven findings, and my favor at less than nothing, and one of my five spent on a door. Tell me why I should keep feeding this."

Cuong knew what the safe answer was. Duy had been two days in the ground.

"Because your quest got them killed," he said. "You posted a rescue to five people who had been murdered that morning, into a dark room, with no words on what was coming, and when it went wrong your System wrote it down in nobody's favor but its own. So keep feeding it or don't. But you watched. You said so. You watched, and you let five terrified people find out what those cells were the hard way, and where I am from, we have a word for an officer who does that with a warrant."

Miss Hue said, softly, in Vietnamese, "Careful, boy."

Odin considered him. Wind moved the fur at his shoulders. Somewhere below the terrace, night birds Cuong had no names for were saying things to each other in a forest older than his species.

"There he is," Odin said at last, to no one, satisfied about something. "The one who stands up. The bird said so and I doubted." He turned and set the spear toward the mountain's shoulder, toward a pass where the pale stone road went up between broken pillars. "Walk with me. All of you. There is a hall at Threshold where the classing is done, and you will want what it gives you, because the court has posted Vietnam's claim, and the claim will not wait for your grief." He paused, and did not turn around. "Nothing waits for grief. That is not the court. That is everything."

They gathered their little gear. At the door of the keeping place, Cuong stopped and looked back once, down the dark corridor toward a doorless cell, and Odin's voice came from up the road without kindness or cruelty, a report from an old front.

"He died holding a gate against a warden twice his weight, laughing," the god said. "In nine thousand years I have furnished halls with less. Now walk."
