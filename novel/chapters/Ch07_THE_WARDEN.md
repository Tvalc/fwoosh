## CHAPTER 7: THE WARDEN

The keys came around the bend before the thing that carried them.

Diep heard them before anything else, filling the corridor. Then the warden came up into the torchlight.

Three meters of gray hide like wet stone. Hands that could palm a door. Grown into the flesh of its left fist, protruding between the knuckles, a ring of iron keys, dozens of them, worn bright with use.

It saw the open cells first. It looked at each of them, one open door at a time, and a sound started low in it that Diep understood with her skin before her ears. The sound was grief. Then the sound was rage.

A screen flickered at the edge of her sight, one line, gone almost before she read it.

> Warden (Bonded). Level 9.

They were level one. All of them.

"Positions," Cuong said, and his voice was the market voice, flat and clean, and her body obeyed it before her terror could vote. "Like we walked it. Duy."

"Yeah." Duy had gone gray again. He was grinning again. He stepped out into the middle of the corridor, in front of the bend, in front of the gate, alone, and cupped both hands to his mouth. "HEY. UGLY. Your zoo's empty."

The warden came like a landslide.

It happened the way Cuong had built it in the dark with torch soot on stone, and it almost worked.

Duy ran. The warden filled the corridor behind him.

Miss Hue stepped out of a cell doorway as it passed and broke its knee with the morningstar, a full turning two-handed strike with ninety years of certainty in it.

The warden stumbled exactly where it was supposed to stumble, under the gate.

Cuong threw his weight on the crank.

The portcullis fell and pinned the warden to the floor and it should have ended there.

It did not end there.

The warden was a keeper of things and knew its own doors. Its free hand found the gate's lower cross-bar and began, impossibly, to lift. The iron came up off its back. Its keyed fist scraped for purchase.

Duy had cleared the drop. He was safe on the far side with them.

He looked at the gate coming up. He looked at Diep. Diep saw him do the thing her brother did, the counting, and she screamed at him not to.

Duy went back under the gate and hit the warden's lifting arm with his whole body.

The gate came down.

It took the warden across the spine. It took Duy across the chest. There was nothing anyone's hands could do about iron.

The warden's yellow eyes went out slowly, like lamps.

Duy's went fast. The last thing in them was aimed at Cuong, and it was the grin, and his mouth made something with no air behind it that had the shape of *tell my mother about Da Lat.*

The screens came very quietly.

> Warden (Bonded): eliminated.
> Your party has been awarded 2,100 experience.

> Companion Duy: deceased.
> This entry is permanent.

That was all. No precedent. No editorial.

Diep put a knife into the warden's dimming eye, because she was terrified it would get up again and nobody else was close enough, and she did it with her face turned away and her whole arm shaking. Then she walked three steps and sat down against the small cells and was sick, nineteen years old, holding her competition knife in the dark under the world.

One more screen came, for Cuong alone, and he read it standing over his partner, and Diep would not learn until much later what it said.

> Estate of Companion Duy: settled.
> Outstanding balance, Caedite Eos: transferred.
> The debts of the dead do not die. Current balance: 19, plus 5.
> The court extends no condolences. The court notes that it has none to extend.
