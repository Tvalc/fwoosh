## CHAPTER 8: THE FIRST DOOR

They buried Duy inside the mountain, which he would have hated, and Diep said so out loud while they dug, because it was the closest thing to teasing him that was left.

It took the four of them most of what felt like a day. The warden's keeping place had a storeroom, and the storeroom had tools sized for its hands, and Cuong dug until his palms tore and would not be relieved, and nobody tried twice.

Halfway through he stopped and stood with his hand on the wall, breathing wrong. Miss Hue went and stood beside him and said nothing at all until he could start again.

They laid Duy in a cell with the door taken off its hinges, because Duy did not sleep behind doors. Miss Hue said words over him in the old way, and then, to Diep's astonishment, said different words, brisk ones, in the tone of a woman lodging a complaint with a specific official, upward.

"Your god's going to hear that," Duy would have said, delighted.

"He had better," Miss Hue said to nobody, and picked up her morningstar.

The corridor past the warden's rooms climbed. It climbed until the wet black stone went dry, and the dry stone went gray, and the gray began, impossibly, to hold light that came from somewhere that was not torches.

A door of plain heavy wood ended it, barred on their side. Cuong lifted the bar, and they walked out of the underside of the world.

Atlantis at dusk went up forever.

They stood on a terrace of pale stone high on a mountainside. Below them forests fell away in shelves toward a plain veined with rivers. Above them, past the shoulder of the mountain, towers stood that no human hand had stacked, joined by bridges thin as written lines. The air tasted of rain and some spice Diep had no name for. Far off, over the plain, something with a wingspan she refused to estimate turned once, unhurried, and slid behind a cloud.

Nobody spoke. There was too much world.

The writing found them there, the way it would always find them.

> **Questline available: BLOOD DEBT.**
> Balance outstanding: 19, plus 5, plus findings.
> Terms of satisfaction: lives preserved at personal risk. The court does not care whose.
> This world is short of no one who needs preserving.
> Accept?

Diep read it in the failing light. It was a door. Numbered and ruled and garnished, and still a door.

She looked at her brother. Cuong stood at the terrace rail with the dusk on his face and the debtor's mark over his head where every eye on this world could read it, and he was looking out at the plain the way he used to look at case files.

"Accept," Diep said.

High above the towers, with a crack that rolled down the mountain like a verdict, black wings the size of a fishing boat unfolded from a spire, and a raven older than her country fell toward them down the dark.
