## cit-001 · Sek-Ra-Tun · Elder · the one who knew all the songs
cohort: cell, non-combatant (Cuong's balance) · visual `elder` · leans Memory
**Wall-mark:** a spiral, and a second spiral inside it. The second means the songs are now unheld. *(Ch14, exact.)*

**Background.** The wall shows what a person's thing was. The songs say what the person was like, and Sek-Ra-Tun held all of them. She never sat anyone down to learn. She sang at the low fire while hands were busy, so that half the village half-knows most of the songs and nobody knows them whole. She thought there was time. The oldest songs are in sounds no one can turn into pictures any more. She sang them anyway, exactly, the way her own teacher had. In the room in the deep she put Nim and Kip behind her and held her arms out. She was still holding them out at the end.

**Wants in sanctuary:** to sing every song through, to everyone, until somebody else holds them.
**Toward Duy:** she will hear his account before she weighs it. She watches whether he learns their names.
**First meeting:** "Sek-Ra-Tun knew all the songs. She is looking for someone to give them to."

## cit-002 · Nim · child, about eight
cohort: cell, non-combatant · visual `child` · no bloc
**Wall-mark:** a beetle. Kesh-ka drew it small.

**Background.** Nim's mother climbs and her father mends, and both of them are alive. Nim knows this because everyone in sanctuary has told her, gently, more than once. It is good news. She is working on how good news can mean she does not get to see them. In the village she rode on the cook's carry-basket, sorted beetle shells by colour and asked what happened next until the grown-ups ran out of answers. In the cells she learned to hide food in her bedding. She was behind her grandmother when the thunder came.

**Wants in sanctuary:** her mother and father, one day, old and happy and in no hurry.
**Toward Duy:** she has not joined the burned man at the gate to the noise in the dark. She asks him things no one else will.
**First meeting:** "Nim is eight. Her mother and father are alive, and she is waiting for them."

## cit-012 · Kip · child, about five
cohort: cell, non-combatant · visual `child` · no bloc
**Wall-mark:** a hammock-cradle, two lines and a curve.

**Background.** Kip's mother died when he was born and his father went into the black spring the winter after. He remembers neither. He remembers Ili-Sa-Ven's back, the smell of new cord and a cradle he would not give up when he grew too long for it. He was the whole village's child, fed at every fire. In the room in the deep Ili-Sa-Ven pushed him to the old grandmother, because the grandmother was further from the tall shapes, and the grandmother held him until the end.

**Wants in sanctuary:** more of today.
**Toward Duy:** he hides. He takes his lead from Ili-Sa-Ven.
**First meeting:** "Kip is five. He is behind Ili-Sa-Ven. If you wave, he may wave back."

**Conditional entries**
- `arrival+cit-010 present` → "He found her by the smell of the cord."
- `community-care:first-season` → "He stayed a whole morning at the children's house. He reported that there were beetles there too."

## cit-010 · Ili-Sa-Ven · the one whose knot never slipped
cohort: cell, non-combatant · visual `weaver` · leans Hearth, holds out
**Wall-mark:** the knot, the hard one from the roof-lashings. *(Ch14, exact. She is Chit-tat-to's aunt.)*

**Background.** Every roof in the root tunnels hangs from Ili-Sa-Ven's lashings, and none of them has ever come down. She is Mor-Ne-Dath's younger sister. When her sister was made an Elder, Ili-Sa-Ven dyed thread red, which took her most of a season, and braided her a cord for her throat. She had no children of her own and had planned none. Then Kip had no one, and the village looked about for who would take him and found she already had. In the room in the deep she passed him back to the old grandmother and turned to face the noise herself. Her sister's boy got out. She holds on to that.

**Wants in sanctuary:** for Kip to grow up safe enough to be bored.
**Toward Duy:** she does not ask that he be punished. She asks that he never come near Kip, and she will hold up the Hearth alone for it.
**First meeting:** "Ili-Sa-Ven ties the knots that hold the roofs up. The small one behind her is Kip. Give them room."

**Conditional entries**
- `home:burrow` → "She hung a cradle, though Kip is too long for it. He sleeps in it anyway, folded small."
- `reunion:cit-013` → "She looked first at her sister's throat. The red cord was there."
- `favor:hearth-earned` → "The Hearth decided. She was heard, and she was outvoted, and she was told so gently. She has not come round. She has stopped standing in the lane."

**Manuscript tie (N-13):** the red cord Cuong sees in Ch06 "made by somebody for somebody" is hers, made for her sister.

## cit-013 · Mor-Ne-Dath · Elder · keeper of the wall
cohort: cell, non-combatant · visual `baker` · leans Bowl
**Wall-mark:** the grinding stone, and at its edge a dot with a curve over it, the mark for one who taught. *(Ch14, exact.)*
**AD-35 applies: nothing here shows her remembering, naming or waiting for her son.**

**Background.** Mor-Ne-Dath sat at the grinding stone, and because the stone is where everyone comes, she was also the one who kept the wall. When someone died she mixed the ash and chose who would draw. She corrected the drawing if it was wrong, and it was often wrong, and she was not soft about it. She taught the same way. A child who brought her a bad mark was sent to do it again, and a child who brought her a good one was given the next harder thing and no praise at all. Half the village can read the old marks because of her. She wore a red cord her sister made. At the end, in the room in the deep, she had a piece of the floor in her hand.

**Wants in sanctuary:** a wall. There is four hundred years of marking to set down again from memory, and she has started a list of who is to draw what.
**Toward Duy:** she is keeping his account the way she kept everyone's. It is not finished, so she has not judged it.
**First meeting:** "Mor-Ne-Dath ground the village's meal and kept its wall. She will tell you if you have drawn it wrong."

**Conditional entries**
- `job:farm-or-food` → "She ground the first meal. She said it was coarse. She ground it again."
- `home:any` → "She chose the room with the longest bare wall."
- `celebration:memorial` → "She mixed ash and drew the first mark on sanctuary's wall. She let no one help, and then she let everyone."

## cit-017 · Shi-Pa-Nol, called Ptik · the one who could take three fish
cohort: cell, non-combatant · visual `farmer` (fisher variant requested) · leans Bowl, toward favor
**Wall-mark:** a fishing line. *(Ch14, exact.)*

**Background.** On a day when no one else could take one fish out of the black spring, Ptik could take three. There was no trick to it that she could teach. She sat still longer than other people were able to, and the fish forgot she was there. She was never in a hurry and she was never late. In the hungry seasons the mash had fish in it because of her, and she did not wait to be asked. Her line went up the drop-shaft with Chit-tat-to. She would have given it to him herself.

**Wants in sanctuary:** deep water, a quiet bank and no reason to move.
**Toward Duy:** he is doing a slow job steadily. She thinks well of that before she thinks well of him.
**First meeting:** "Everyone calls her Ptik. She fishes. She will get there."

**Conditional entries**
- `arrival` → "She came up the road last of her group by some way, and was not troubled."
- `production:first-food` → "There was fish in the pot. Nobody had seen her go."
