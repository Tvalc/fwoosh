# Fwoosh story spine (batch 22) · built from Tony's interview, AD-42…AD-53

What Tony asked for, in his ranked order: **(1) characters who grow by making hard promises and keeping them, (2) magic with clear rules, costs and limits, (3) clues planted early that pay off in a rush, (4) plain prose, (5) lived-in culture.** Touchstone: *Mistborn*. Sentence-level voice still follows `blood-debt-writing` (Griffin for Duy, Brooks for Ratkin, the Auditor for System text).

## 1. Duy's arc (AD-47, AD-48, AD-49)
- **The lie he starts with:** a debt can be paid off and closed. He is a cop who keeps accounts straight; Diep still owes him corn. He enters the fire to clear a balance.
- **The truth:** some debts you carry for good, and carrying is the point.
- **First promise, cheap:** to Nim at the gate, before he knows the price. "All the ones on there." (`asked`)
- **The price arrives:** he sees Cuong older and scarred and learns every run has cost a day. (`days`)
- **Second promise, dear:** to Khet-Tak-Tor, knowing the cost. He will stay until the wall is empty; if released first he will go to Cuong and **return to the fire every night in his sleep** until it is done. (`days`)
- **Keeping it:** release comes before the fire is empty. He goes. He comes back. The side quest stays playable after release, and that is the promise being kept.

## 2. The rules of the fire (AD-07, AD-10, AD-45, AD-46). Nothing in any text may break these.
1. Every Ratkin dead burns until carried through. Carried through means lifted up. If the fire goes dark with anyone inside, they are gone.
2. Carrying moves the fire from them into the carrier. It hurts, and enough of it kills him.
3. **Weight:** the longer someone has burned, the more they cost to carry. The twenty-four are light. The wall's oldest are the heaviest.
4. **Growth:** each carry raises the carrier's Constitution a little. This is the only way either carrier grows stronger.
5. **Time:** one run is one **game day** among the living. No exceptions. The Auditor's death notice has said so since run one: "Death recorded. One day entered."
6. **Scope (AD-42):** Duy's promise covers this village's dead: the twenty-four and everyone on the four-hundred-year wall. Finite, and every one is authored. Other worlds' dead are Khet-Tak-Tor's and come later.
7. Khet-Tak-Tor is bound by the same rules. He can manage one ordinary carry. He could not survive the oldest.

Why these make a Mistborn ending: rules 3, 4 and 6 mean the last person out is the oldest on the wall, the heaviest carry in the game, reachable only by a Duy who has grown through every earlier carry. Rule 5 means he pays for that growth in days Cuong fights without him.

## 3. The twist (AD-43, AD-44, AD-50)
- The wall's first mark was drawn by **the first keeper of the first wall**, an ordinary Ratkin who lived in the kingdom and drew its name. Nobody grand.
- The name is **Atlantis**. The Ratkin were its first people. The Authors and their System came later and were built over them. They are not the Authors.
- What it reframes: the System rates the Ratkin "barely sentient" and gives the discarded races no standing to claim land. The oldest claim on the map belongs to them.
- **Delivery by route (AD-44):** inside Ledger, Fwoosh players learn it early and Ledger reveals it on its own later so nobody misses it. In standalone Fwoosh (mobile, CrazyGames, itch) it unlocks as the end of standalone progression.
- Already planted: the oldest songs are in sounds no one can turn into pictures (cit-001). As older ancestors arrive, each corrects the song from their own lifetime (AD-16). Mor-Ne-Dath is rebuilding the wall from memory and cannot do the first mark (cit-013). Ch14: the pipe was "laid by somebody long dead"; the deep tunnels below the spring have not been opened in three generations.

## 6. `name`: beats (prose next batch)
1. Release has come and gone. Duy is asleep somewhere on a road in Atlantis and awake in the street. The wall in sanctuary has one bare place at its start.
2. Khet-Tak-Tor tells him flatly that this one has burned longest of any of them and that he, Khet-Tak-Tor, tried once and was thrown back.
3. The carry. It costs more than any before. Procedure, not poetry: what he does with his hands, how far, how many times he goes down.
4. She rises. She is small, a wall-keeper with ash on her fingers. In sanctuary Mor-Ne-Dath hands her the ash without a word.
5. She draws the first mark. Sek-Ra-Tun sings the first song and for the first time the sounds are a word. The Auditor renders it, reluctantly exact.
6. Duy recognises it. It is on every map he has seen since he died.
7. Last line is an action: he goes back down, because there is usually someone calling.
